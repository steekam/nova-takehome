# Classes of Fire — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Sub-strand 1.3 · Suggested teaching allocation: 20 lessons. Total: 30 marks. Use three paper-based instruments: a fire-principles check, response scenarios, and a safety communication task. Allow oral answers, written answers, or accessible poster descriptions. No learner lights a fire, discharges an extinguisher, handles fuel, or tests a live electrical appliance.

For the classification tasks, supply this key: A—ordinary combustible solids such as paper, wood, and cloth; B—flammable liquids or gases; C—energized electrical equipment; D—combustible metals. These labels follow the Kenyan sources listed below; labels can differ between standards. Actual extinguisher selection follows its rating, the workplace plan, and trained adult instruction. Learners’ immediate actions are to raise the alarm and move to safety.

## Instrument A — Fire principles, 15 minutes, 13 marks

### A1. Identify a cause — 4 marks
Outcome 1.3(a). **Task:** Name a possible ignition cause in each scene and one action that reduces risk or harm: dry grass struck by lightning; an overloaded extension socket with damaged insulation. **Expected answer:** Lightning can ignite dry vegetation; keep people away/report the fire and manage vegetation under adult guidance. Electrical overheating or a fault can ignite nearby material; report damage, avoid overloading, and have an authorized adult isolate/repair it. **Scoring:** 1 per cause and 1 per suitable risk-reduction action. **Feedback:** Distinguish the ignition cause from the fuel that burns. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

### A2. Complete the fire triangle — 3 marks
Outcome 1.3(b). **Task:** Name the three components required for a fire to continue. **Expected answer:** Fuel, heat, and oxygen. **Scoring:** 1 per component. Accept “air supplying oxygen,” but not air alone if the learner cannot identify oxygen. **Feedback:** Removing one required component can interrupt burning; it does not make every attempted removal safe. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

### A3. Explain a control principle — 3 marks
Outcomes 1.3(b), 1.3(c). **Task:** In a training video, a trained adult uses a suitable fire blanket to cover a very small contained fire. Which triangle component is restricted? Explain why lifting the blanket too soon is unsafe. **Expected answer:** The cover restricts oxygen; lifting it can admit oxygen while fuel remains hot and allow the fire to restart. **Scoring:** 1 for oxygen, 1 for remaining heat/fuel, 1 for possible renewed burning. **Feedback:** Explain the mechanism rather than saying only “the blanket puts it out.” Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

### A4. Trace the danger — 3 marks
Outcome 1.3(d). **Task:** State three different harms a grassland fire can cause to people, other living things, or the environment. **Expected answer:** Any three distinct valid harms, such as burns or smoke injury, loss of homes/crops, death or displacement of wildlife, habitat loss, or damaged soil/vegetation. **Scoring:** 1 per distinct harm; repeated wording of the same harm earns one mark. **Feedback:** Show the range of consequences rather than listing three versions of injury. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

## Instrument B — Classify and respond, 20 minutes, 11 marks

### B1. Use the classification key — 4 marks
Outcome 1.3(c). **Task:** Using the supplied key, classify fires involving (i) a stack of paper, (ii) a petrol spill, (iii) a powered electrical cabinet, and (iv) burning magnesium. **Expected answer:** A, B, C, D respectively. **Scoring:** 1 per correct classification. This assesses use of the stated scheme; do not penalize a learner for noting that another taught standard uses different labels. **Feedback:** Classify the burning material and any energized electrical hazard before considering equipment. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18; Kenyan fire references below.

### B2. Choose safe control reasoning — 4 marks
Outcome 1.3(c). **Task:** Explain why (i) throwing water on powered electrical equipment is unsafe, and (ii) a burning petrol spill needs a trained adult using equipment rated for that fire rather than a learner’s improvised response. **Expected answer:** Water may conduct electricity and expose people to shock; an unsuitable agent or action can spread burning liquid or expose the person to flame/smoke. **Scoring:** 2 per explanation: 1 for the hazard and 1 for a safe response—raise alarm, evacuate, and inform a trained adult. **Feedback:** A correct class name does not make a learner equipped to fight a fire. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

### B3. Read an equipment label — 3 marks
Outcomes 1.3(c), 1.3(d). **Task:** A school extinguisher label lists its permitted fire classes and warns “use only after training.” Give three checks or actions before anyone considers its use. **Expected evidence:** Raise alarm/ensure escape route; confirm a trained adult is responsible; check the fire is within the labelled rating and conditions; follow the school emergency plan. **Scoring:** 1 per distinct sound point, up to 3. Do not award an action that delays evacuation or sends a learner back toward danger. **Feedback:** Equipment labels and an escape route are part of the decision. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

## Instrument C — Safety communication, 20 minutes, 6 marks

### C1. Produce a clear safety message — 6 marks
Outcomes 1.3(a), 1.3(b), 1.3(c), 1.3(d). **Task:** Design a one-page poster or give a one-minute accessible announcement for a school store containing paper and electrical equipment. Include a likely cause, the fire triangle, one prevention action, and what learners should do if they see smoke. **Expected evidence:** Accurate cause/triangle, feasible prevention, alarm and evacuation instructions, and a clear danger message. **Scoring:** cause 1; three triangle components 1; prevention 1; alarm and evacuation 2; clear, relevant danger message 1. Do not score drawing ability. **Feedback:** Make the action words easy to find and avoid instructions that invite learners to fight a fire. Source: KICD table 1.3, printed pp. 5–6 / PDF pp. 17–18.

## Coverage and marking record

1.3(a): A1, C1. 1.3(b): A2, A3, C1. 1.3(c): A3, B1–B3, C1. 1.3(d): A4, B3, C1. Instrument totals: A = 13, B = 11, C = 6; pack total = 30. Assess the poster’s stated decisions and evidence, without inferring a learner’s private attitude toward danger.
