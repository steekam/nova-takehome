# Reproduction in Human Beings — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Strand 2.0: Living Things and their Environment · Sub-strand 2.3. Curriculum reference: KICD, *Integrated Science Grade 8 - July 2024*, table 2.3, printed pages 13–14 / PDF pages 25–26. Outcome references use the sub-strand number and printed letter to link to catalogue targets. Keep teaching and assessment within the design's limits: no hormonal-control detail, no detailed blastocyst formation, and no causative-agent detail for the named STIs.

Use a 15-minute cycle check (8 marks), a 25-minute planning and sequence activity (12 marks), and a 20-minute health-information check (16 marks). Total: 36 marks. Use fictional cases and labelled diagrams. Learners may answer in writing, by arranging cards, or orally in a private teacher conference. Do not ask for personal menstrual, sexual or health histories. Set respectful discussion rules, accept an anonymous question box, and provide a private way to seek support from a trusted adult or health worker. Judge the science and the plan's practicality; do not score personal beliefs or disclosure.

## Instrument A — Menstrual cycle and challenges

### A1. Organise a cycle model — 4 marks
Outcome: 2.3(a); locator: table 2.3(a), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** Arrange these cards into a repeating cycle: “an egg is released”; “the uterine lining is shed as menstruation”; “the lining builds up again”; “if pregnancy does not occur, the lining is later shed.” Mark the start used for counting cycle days and explain why a diagram labelled 28 days is a model. **Expected:** Counting starts on the first day of menstruation; rebuilding precedes egg release, followed by the next shedding if pregnancy does not occur. Cycle length and timing vary. **Scoring:** 2 for a sensible cyclic order, 1 for day-one identification, 1 for variation. Do not require a fixed day of ovulation. **Feedback:** Ask what repeats and why a single calendar cannot describe every person's cycle.

### A2. Recognise challenges — 4 marks
Outcome: 2.3(a); locator: table 2.3(a), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** A fictional learner reports cramps, unpredictable dates and bleeding that disrupts normal activities. Name two challenges shown, then give two respectful ways classmates or school staff can support the learner. **Expected:** Pain/cramps, irregular periods or troublesome bleeding; support may include privacy, access to suitable products and water, a safe rest space, help catching up, or access to a trusted adult/health worker. **Scoring:** 1 per valid challenge and 1 per practical support action, maximum 4. Avoid judging severity from this short case or suggesting treatment. **Feedback:** Ask which action helps participation while protecting privacy.

## Instrument B — Plan and explain

### B1. Build a management plan — 6 marks
Outcomes: 2.3(b), 2.3(f); locator: table 2.3(b), 2.3(f), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** Write a six-step school-day plan for the fictional learner in A2. Cover preparation, hygiene, privacy, learning participation and seeking help when symptoms are severe, persistent or worrying. **Expected evidence:** A workable plan includes suitable clean menstrual products, safe changing and handwashing, appropriate disposal or cleaning, discreet access to supplies/rest/toilets, a way to track dates or concerns, and a trusted adult or health worker support route. **Scoring:** 1 per distinct practical step, maximum 6; a vague “be clean” earns no point without an action. Accept suitable reusable or disposable products. **Feedback:** Ask what the learner can do before, during and after a difficult school day.

### B2. Fertilisation to implantation — 6 marks
Outcome: 2.3(c); locator: table 2.3(c), printed 13 / PDF 25; scope limits, printed 13–14 / PDF 25–26. **Task:** On a simple labelled outline of uterus and oviducts, place these labels and explain their order: sperm and egg meet; fertilisation; early developing embryo travels; implantation in the uterine lining. State where fertilisation and implantation normally occur. **Expected:** Fertilisation is sperm–egg fusion, usually in an oviduct. The early embryo moves towards the uterus and implants in its lining. **Scoring:** 2 for sites, 2 for correct sequence, 1 for fusion, 1 for implantation meaning. No detail on blastocyst formation is needed. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to distinguish the meeting site from the later attachment site.

## Instrument C — Use reproductive-health information

### C1. Outline possible STI presentations — 6 marks
Outcome: 2.3(d); locator: table 2.3(d), printed 13 / PDF 25; named STIs, printed 14 / PDF 26. **Task:** For HIV, gonorrhoea, syphilis and genital herpes, state one possible symptom or presentation. Add a sentence explaining the limit of using symptoms to identify an infection. **Expected:** Accept HIV with no symptoms for a long time or an early flu-like illness; gonorrhoea with discharge or painful urination; syphilis with a painless sore or later rash; herpes with painful blisters/sores. Many infections have no symptoms, and symptoms alone cannot confirm a diagnosis. **Scoring:** 1 per valid presentation, 1 for asymptomatic infection, 1 for need for appropriate health assessment/testing. Do not ask learners to diagnose a person. **Feedback:** Ask why looking well cannot establish that someone has no infection.

### C2. Choose a response to concern — 2 marks
Outcomes: 2.3(d), 2.3(f); locator: table 2.3(d), 2.3(f), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** A fictional young person notices an unexplained sore and asks a friend to identify it from a photograph. Give two safer actions. **Expected:** Seek confidential help from a qualified health worker or a trusted adult who can help access care; avoid sharing private images or making a diagnosis from appearance. **Scoring:** 1 per distinct suitable action, maximum 2. **Feedback:** Ask which person has the training and privacy arrangements to assess the concern.

### C3. Explain prevention measures — 4 marks
Outcome: 2.3(e); locator: table 2.3(e), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** Give two measures that reduce STI risk and explain how each helps. **Expected:** Accept abstaining from sexual contact; correct and consistent condom use for sexually active people, with protection limits; avoiding shared needles or unsterile skin-piercing tools; and appropriate testing/health care that helps identify and manage infection. **Scoring:** 1 for each valid measure and 1 for each linked explanation, maximum 4. Do not credit washing after sex as reliable prevention or a claim that condoms remove every risk. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to connect the measure to a route of transmission rather than use a slogan.

### C4. A respectful health message — 4 marks
Outcomes: 2.3(e), 2.3(f); locator: table 2.3(e–f), printed 13 / PDF 25. **Task:** Rewrite this message for a school notice: “You can identify anyone with an STI by looking at them. Avoid sitting with them.” Include two accurate health points and one supportive action. **Expected:** Infections can be asymptomatic; appearance cannot diagnose infection; ordinary social contact such as sitting together does not transmit the named STIs; a supportive action protects privacy and encourages qualified help. **Scoring:** 2 for accurate corrected points, 1 for a respectful action, 1 for a clear non-stigmatising message. **Feedback:** Ask how the revised notice can protect both health and a person's dignity.

## Coverage and recording

2.3(a): A1, A2. 2.3(b): B1. 2.3(c): B2. 2.3(d): C1, C2. 2.3(e): C3, C4. 2.3(f): A2, B1, C2, C4. Instrument totals: A 8 + B 12 + C 16 = 36 marks. Score evidence in the fictional tasks; do not infer a learner's health status, sexual experience or private attitudes.
