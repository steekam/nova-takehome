# Movement of Materials in and out of the Cell — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Strand 2.0: Living Things and their Environment · Sub-strand 2.2. Curriculum reference: KICD, *Integrated Science Grade 8 - July 2024*, table 2.2, printed pages 11–12 / PDF pages 23–24. Outcome references such as 2.2(a) combine the sub-strand number and printed letter and link to the catalogue targets.

Use the three instruments during the sub-strand: a 15-minute concept check (8 marks), a supervised practical session with observations over time (14 marks), and a 20-minute application check (18 marks). Total: 40 marks. Materials: potato strips and thin slices cut by the teacher, water, sugar solution, cups, labels, balance, ruler, paper towels, and teacher-prepared iodine stain. Use equal-sized potato pieces, the same liquid volume and temperature, and a fixed immersion time. Learners must not taste materials. The teacher handles cutting and stain; protect eyes and clothing and wash hands. Provide large-print data and oral response options. A learner may direct a peer or teacher where manipulation is difficult; record practical and explanation evidence separately.

## Instrument A — Concept check

### A1. A concentration story — 4 marks
Outcome: 2.2(a); locator: table 2.2(a), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** A cell is surrounded by a solution containing many more dissolved food molecules than the cell interior. The molecules can pass through its membrane. Describe their net movement at first and explain why movement later becomes balanced. **Expected:** Net movement is into the cell, from higher to lower concentration. Molecules keep moving randomly; at balance, movement in both directions continues at equal overall rates. **Scoring:** 1 for inward net movement, 1 for the concentration difference, 1 for continued random movement, 1 for equal movement at balance. Accept equivalent wording. **Feedback:** If the learner says molecules stop, ask what continues to happen on both sides of the membrane.

### A2. Identify osmosis — 4 marks
Outcome: 2.2(a); locator: table 2.2(a), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** Two solutions are separated by a membrane that allows water through but prevents sugar passing. Side P has less sugar per equal volume than side Q. Draw an arrow for the net water movement and explain why this movement is osmosis. **Expected:** Net water movement is P to Q: water passes through a partially permeable membrane from the more dilute to the more concentrated solution. **Scoring:** 1 for the correct arrow, 1 for water as the moving substance, 1 for the membrane condition, 1 for the concentration explanation. Do not credit an arrow showing sugar movement. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to name both the moving substance and the membrane feature.

## Instrument B — Plant-material practical

### B1. Observe diffusion into plant tissue — 6 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(a), 2.2(b); locator: table 2.2(a–b), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** The teacher supplies matched thin potato slices. Place one in water and one in dilute iodine stain for the same time; the teacher then cuts each to expose a fresh interior. Record colour at the edge and centre, compare the control, and explain the movement observed. **Expected evidence:** An accurate labelled record; iodine colour/starch reaction appears progressively within exposed tissue, while the water control does not show it. The explanation links net iodine movement into tissue to a concentration difference. **Scoring:** 2 for fair setup and labels, 2 for recorded comparisons, 2 for a supported diffusion explanation. Do not claim this alone proves transport through intact cell membranes. **Feedback:** Ask which observation supports movement and which comparison rules out the potato's original colour.

### B2. Demonstrate osmosis in potato — 8 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(a), 2.2(b); locator: table 2.2(a–b), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** Measure and blot two matched potato strips. Put one in water and one in a concentrated sugar solution for the teacher's fixed time. Blot again; measure mass or length and compare firmness. Present a labelled result table and explain each change. **Expected evidence:** A fair comparison and honest measurements. The water strip usually gains mass and becomes firmer; the sugar-solution strip usually loses mass and becomes less firm as water moves through cell membranes. **Scoring:** 2 for controlled setup, 2 for initial/final records, 2 for observations, 2 for directions of water movement linked to concentration. Credit a well-recorded unexpected result with a sensible limitation; do not reward invented results. **Feedback:** Ask why blotting must be consistent before both measurements.

## Instrument C — Use the evidence

### C1. Read an osmosis result — 4 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(a), 2.2(b); locator: table 2.2(a–b), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** Both strips started at 5.0 g. After equal times, the strip in water measured 5.6 g and the strip in concentrated sugar solution measured 4.4 g. State the net water direction for each strip and use the measurements as evidence. **Expected:** Water entered the first strip, shown by its 0.6 g gain; water left the second, shown by its 0.6 g loss. **Scoring:** 1 per direction and 1 per matching measurement/change. No percentage calculation is required. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to connect each claim to a before-and-after value.

### C2. Match movement to a role — 6 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(c), 2.2(d); locator: table 2.2(c–d), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** Explain the relevant movement in each case: water entering a root-hair cell from dilute soil water; oxygen moving from air in a lung air sac into blood where its concentration is lower; carbon dioxide moving from a respiring cell to its surroundings where its concentration is lower. **Expected:** Osmosis supplies water to the root cell; diffusion moves oxygen into blood; diffusion removes carbon dioxide. Each explanation identifies the stated concentration difference and a useful role. **Scoring:** 1 for the process and 1 for its role in each case. Do not describe mineral salts as moving by osmosis. **Feedback:** Ask whether the moving substance is water alone or a different substance.

### C3. Explain a wilted plant — 4 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(c), 2.2(d); locator: table 2.2(c–d), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** A watered plant stands in very salty soil and wilts. Explain one possible reason and propose one observation that would help investigate it. **Expected:** A concentrated external solution can cause water to leave root cells by osmosis, reducing cell firmness. A useful observation compares plant condition or soil solution with a similar plant in less salty soil while other conditions are held alike. **Scoring:** 2 for the water-movement explanation, 1 for a relevant comparison, 1 for a controlled condition. Accept other scientifically sound investigations. **Feedback:** Ask why adding more dissolved salt may change water movement even when soil looks wet.

### C4. Compare the processes — 4 marks
Outcomes: 2.2(a), 2.2(d); locator: table 2.2(a), 2.2(d), printed 11 / PDF 23. **Task:** Write two sentences comparing diffusion and osmosis, then explain why a living organism needs these movements. **Expected:** Both involve net movement down a concentration difference. Osmosis concerns water through a partially permeable membrane; diffusion can involve other particles and need not involve a membrane. A valid role includes water supply, gas exchange, or movement of suitable dissolved substances. **Scoring:** 1 for the shared feature, 2 for the distinction, 1 for a valid biological role. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to include the membrane and the substance in the osmosis sentence.

## Coverage and recording

2.2(a): A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C4. 2.2(b): B1, B2, C1; use B1/B2 observations for performance evidence. 2.2(c): C2, C3. 2.2(d): C2, C3, C4. Instrument totals: A 8 + B 14 + C 18 = 40 marks. Record practical procedure, observation and explanation separately so a weak written response does not erase demonstrated skill.
