# Elements and Compounds — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Sub-strand 1.1 · Suggested teaching allocation: 18 lessons. Use the short check during teaching, the label task after learners have examined products, and the application check near the end of the sub-strand. Total: 30 marks. Learners may write, dictate, or give recorded oral responses; assess scientific meaning rather than spelling except when a chemical symbol is the target.

Prepare clean empty product containers or use the label text supplied below. Do not open, taste, or mix products. Give learners the selected-element symbol list used in teaching only after the short check. Outcome references such as 1.1(a) join the sub-strand number to its printed outcome letter; the catalogue retains the full outcome statements.

## Instrument A — Short check, 15 minutes, 12 marks

### A1. Building blocks — 4 marks
Outcome 1.1(a). **Task:** Match each description to atom, element, molecule, or compound: (i) the smallest particle of an element that can take part in chemical change; (ii) a substance containing only one kind of atom; (iii) two or more atoms joined together; (iv) a substance with atoms of different elements chemically joined. **Expected answer:** (i) atom; (ii) element; (iii) molecule; (iv) compound. **Scoring:** 1 per correct match. Accept equivalent wording in an oral response. **Feedback:** A molecule can contain the same element, as in oxygen; a compound contains different elements. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

### A2. Element symbols — 6 marks
Outcome 1.1(b). **Task:** Write the symbols for oxygen, carbon, sodium, iron, copper, and chlorine. **Expected answer:** O, C, Na, Fe, Cu, Cl. **Scoring:** 1 each; capitalization must distinguish a capital first letter from a lower-case second letter. Do not award a symbol with incorrect letters. **Feedback:** Several symbols come from older names; learn the correct pair rather than guessing from the English name. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

### A3. A compound forms — 2 marks
Outcome 1.1(c). **Task:** Copper reacts with oxygen to form copper oxide. Write a word equation. **Expected answer:** copper + oxygen → copper oxide. **Scoring:** 1 for the two reactants; 1 for the product on the right of an arrow or an equivalent “forms” sign. A sentence repeating the prompt earns no equation mark. **Feedback:** Put starting substances on the left and the substance formed on the right. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

## Instrument B — Packaging and uses, 20 minutes, 10 marks

### B1. Read the labels — 4 marks
Outcomes 1.1(a), 1.1(e). **Task:** Label A reads “table salt: sodium chloride.” Label B reads “rehydration sachet: glucose, sodium chloride, potassium chloride; dissolve the whole sachet in the stated volume of clean water.” Name two compounds from these labels and name the two elements in sodium chloride. **Expected answer:** Any two of sodium chloride, glucose, potassium chloride; sodium and chlorine. **Scoring:** 1 per compound and 1 per element. Do not accept “sodium chloride” as an element. **Feedback:** A compound name can help identify its constituent elements, although every name does not show all of them. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

### B2. Choose a material — 4 marks
Outcome 1.1(d). **Task:** Match copper, aluminium, iron, and carbon in graphite to these uses: electrical wire, kitchen foil, strong building nails, and the writing core of a pencil. **Expected answer:** copper—wire; aluminium—foil; iron—nails; graphite/carbon—pencil core. **Scoring:** 1 per correct match. Accept another defensible everyday use of the same element if the learner explains it. **Feedback:** Connect a use with a useful property, such as conductivity, strength, or ease of shaping. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

### B3. A label-reading decision — 2 marks
Outcome 1.1(e). **Task:** Before using a packaged drink powder, list two pieces of label information you would check and explain how one affects your decision. **Expected evidence:** Relevant checks such as ingredients, mixing instructions, expiry date, or storage advice, with a sensible consequence. **Scoring:** 1 for two relevant checks; 1 for a clear reason, for example following the stated water volume or avoiding an expired product. Do not score whether the learner likes the product. **Feedback:** Useful label reading leads to a specific action. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

## Instrument C — Apply the ideas, 15 minutes, 8 marks

### C1. Two word equations — 4 marks
Outcome 1.1(c). **Task:** Write word equations for (i) sodium reacting with chlorine to make sodium chloride, and (ii) hydrogen reacting with oxygen to make water. **Expected answer:** sodium + chlorine → sodium chloride; hydrogen + oxygen → water. **Scoring:** 2 per equation: 1 for correct reactants and 1 for the correct product. Word equations do not require numerical balancing. **Feedback:** Check that each product is a compound formed from the named elements. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

### C2. Explain two particle models — 4 marks
Outcome 1.1(a). **Task:** Model X shows pairs of identical oxygen atoms joined together. Model Y shows two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom. Explain why both show molecules, and why only Y represents a compound. **Expected answer:** Each model has joined atoms; X has only oxygen, while Y contains two different elements chemically joined. **Scoring:** 1 for joined atoms in each model, 1 for X containing one element, and 1 for Y containing different elements. **Feedback:** “Molecule” describes joined atoms; “compound” also requires different elements. Source: KICD table 1.1, printed pp. 1–2 / PDF pp. 13–14.

## Coverage and marking record

1.1(a): A1, B1, C2. 1.1(b): A2. 1.1(c): A3, C1. 1.1(d): B2. 1.1(e): B1, B3. Instrument totals: A = 12, B = 10, C = 8; pack total = 30. Use B3 to record the learner’s observable label-reading decision, without inferring a private attitude.
