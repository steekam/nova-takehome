# Pressure — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Strand 3.0: Force and Energy · Sub-strand 3.2. Curriculum reference: KICD, *Integrated Science Grade 8 - July 2024*, table 3.2, printed pages 19–20 / PDF pages 31–32. Outcome references such as 3.2(a) combine the sub-strand number and printed letter and link to catalogue targets. Keep liquid-pressure work qualitative.

Use a 15-minute prediction check (8 marks), a supervised practical lesson (16 marks), and a 20-minute application check (12 marks). Total: 36 marks. Materials: a rectangular block, soft sponge or damp sand tray, matched loads, teacher-prepared plastic water bottle with small holes, tray, water, and two connected needle-free syringes. The teacher makes holes and checks equipment. Use stable, light loads and wipe spills; do not use glass, sharp tools, mouths on tubing, compressed air or needles. Offer a teacher demonstration, tactile models and oral explanations where needed. Record actual manipulation separately from interpretation.

## Instrument A — Predict and explain

### A1. One block, two faces — 4 marks
Outcome: 3.2(a); locator: table 3.2(a), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** The same rectangular block rests first on its broad face and then on its narrow face in the same soft sand. Predict which position leaves the deeper mark and explain. **Expected:** The narrow face usually makes the deeper mark because the same weight acts over a smaller contact area, producing greater pressure. **Scoring:** 1 for the prediction, 1 for unchanged weight, 1 for smaller area, 1 for the linked pressure explanation. **Feedback:** Ask what changed and what stayed the same when the block was turned.

### A2. Same face, more load — 4 marks
Outcome: 3.2(a); locator: table 3.2(a), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** A block remains on the same face. A matched load is placed on top without changing the contact area. Predict the mark in a fresh patch of the same sand and explain how this compares with A1. **Expected:** The increased load usually creates a deeper mark: greater force/weight acts on the same area. A1 changed area while A2 changes the load. **Scoring:** 1 for prediction, 1 for force/weight link, 1 for fixed area, 1 for correct comparison. **Feedback:** Ask why a fair test should change only one of load and area at a time.

## Instrument B — Investigate pressure

### B1. Demonstrate solid pressure — 6 marks
Outcome: 3.2(a); locator: table 3.2(a), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** Use a light rectangular block and sponge or damp sand to compare two contact areas with the same load. Show a fair setup, record the resulting indentations and explain the pattern. **Expected evidence:** The learner keeps load and surface alike, changes orientation/contact area, records rather than invents results, and links greater indentation on the smaller face to greater pressure. **Scoring:** 2 for controlled setup, 2 for clear comparative observations, 2 for interpretation. Credit an honest unexpected result with a sensible limitation. **Feedback:** Ask how uneven sand or a tilted block could affect the evidence.

### B2. Compare water pressure with depth — 6 marks
Outcome: 3.2(a); locator: table 3.2(a), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** Observe a teacher-prepared bottle with equal small holes at three heights. With water above them, compare the streams close to their openings. Draw and label observations, then explain them qualitatively. **Expected:** The lower hole has a greater height of water above it and greater liquid pressure, so water initially exits more forcefully. As the level falls, the streams weaken. Landing distance alone is an unreliable comparison because holes have different heights above the tray. **Scoring:** 2 for labelled observations, 2 for the depth/height explanation, 1 for changing water level, 1 for a limitation such as unequal holes or different fall heights. Do not use a formula. **Feedback:** Ask whether depth means distance below the current water surface or height above the tray.

### B3. Improve the comparison — 4 marks
Outcome: 3.2(a); locator: table 3.2(a), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** A learner compares water jets from two bottles, but one hole is wider and one bottle contains oil. Identify two changes that would make a better comparison of liquid height alone, and explain why. **Expected:** Use equal holes and the same liquid; keep bottle shape/setup and observation method alike, then vary only water height above the hole. **Scoring:** 1 per relevant controlled change and 1 per linked explanation, maximum 4. **Feedback:** Ask which differences could change a jet even if the liquid heights were identical.

## Instrument C — Explain useful applications

### C1. Select a useful contact area — 4 marks
Outcomes: 3.2(b), 3.2(c); locator: table 3.2(b–c), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** Explain why a wide bag strap can be more comfortable than a narrow strap carrying the same bag, and why a cutting edge is narrow. **Expected:** The wide strap spreads the same load over more area, reducing pressure on the shoulder. The narrow edge concentrates force over a small area, increasing pressure to cut. **Scoring:** 2 for each linked explanation; naming “pressure” alone earns no point. **Feedback:** Ask whether the designer wants to reduce or increase pressure in each case.

### C2. A water-filled syringe model — 4 marks
Outcome: 3.2(b); locator: table 3.2(b), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** Observe two needle-free syringes joined by water-filled tubing. When one plunger is pressed, the other moves. Explain what the model shows and name one related application. **Expected:** Pressure applied to the enclosed liquid is transmitted through it, allowing movement at the other plunger. Hydraulic brakes or a hydraulic lifting device is a valid related application. **Scoring:** 2 for pressure transmission, 1 for the observed movement link, 1 for a suitable application. Do not claim the model creates energy or always increases force. **Feedback:** Ask how trapped air or a leaking connection could change the model's behaviour.

### C3. Make a design recommendation — 4 marks
Outcomes: 3.2(b), 3.2(c); locator: table 3.2(b–c), printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. **Task:** A club must move a heavy box across soft ground. Compare narrow feet with broad support pads, recommend one, and give a second everyday example using the same pressure idea. **Expected:** Broad pads reduce pressure by spreading the load and are less likely to sink. Accept a related example such as wide tyres, snowshoes, broad foundations or a wide strap, with a correct explanation. **Scoring:** 1 for recommendation, 1 for area/load reasoning, 1 for a valid example, 1 for its explanation. **Feedback:** Ask which evidence from the block experiment supports the recommendation.

## Coverage and recording

3.2(a): A1, A2, B1, B2, B3; B1/B2 supply demonstration evidence. 3.2(b): C1, C2, C3. 3.2(c): C1, C3. Instrument totals: A 8 + B 16 + C 12 = 36 marks. Record the learner's procedure, observations and reasoning separately. Use the design choices as evidence of applying pressure ideas in useful contexts.
