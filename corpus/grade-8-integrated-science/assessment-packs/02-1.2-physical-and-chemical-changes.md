# Physical and Chemical Changes — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Sub-strand 1.2 · Suggested teaching allocation: 22 lessons. Total: 30 marks. Use the particle check during teaching, the data investigation after work on heating curves, and the application tasks at the end. Learners may describe diagrams orally or use tactile particle counters. Keep the particle size constant when modelling different states.

This pack uses supplied observations and data. Learners do not heat substances, burn materials, or handle hot apparatus. If a teacher later substitutes a demonstration, follow the school laboratory procedure and retain teacher control of the heat source. Outcome references join the sub-strand number to the printed outcome letter.

## Instrument A — Particles and evidence, 15 minutes, 8 marks

### A1. Compare three states — 6 marks
Outcome 1.2(a). **Task:** Describe particle spacing and movement in a solid, a liquid, and a gas. **Expected answer:** Solid particles are closely packed and vibrate about fixed positions; liquid particles are close together and move past one another; gas particles are far apart and move freely in different directions. **Scoring:** 1 for spacing and 1 for movement in each state. Do not credit claims that gas particles grow larger or that solid particles do not move. **Feedback:** A change of state changes arrangement and movement, not the size of each particle. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

### A2. Ink in still water — 2 marks
Outcome 1.2(a). **Task:** A drop of ink slowly spreads through still water without stirring. Explain the observation using particles. **Expected answer:** Ink and water particles are moving; ink particles spread from the region where they are concentrated into surrounding water. **Scoring:** 1 for particle movement; 1 for spreading from higher to lower concentration. “Water pulls the ink” earns no explanation mark. **Feedback:** Diffusion can occur without visible bulk movement of the water. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

## Instrument B — Data investigation, 20 minutes, 12 marks

### B1. Impurities and temperature — 4 marks
Outcome 1.2(b). **Task:** In one controlled investigation at the same pressure, pure water boils at 100°C and salt solution at 102°C; pure ice melts at 0°C and the tested salt–ice mixture begins melting at −5°C. State the effect of added salt in each comparison, quote one pair of readings to support a claim, and name one variable that must be controlled. **Expected answer:** Salt raises the boiling temperature in this test and lowers the melting temperature; for example, water boils at 100°C and the salt solution at 102°C. A suitable control includes pressure, thermometer method, or comparable sample quantity. **Scoring:** 1 per direction, 1 for a correct paired comparison, and 1 for a valid control. **Feedback:** Describe the measured comparison; do not assume every impurity has the same numerical effect. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

### B2. Read a heating record — 4 marks
Outcome 1.2(a). **Task:** Pure liquid water at standard atmospheric pressure has these readings while heated steadily: 0 min/20°C; 2/40; 4/60; 6/80; 8/100; 10/100; 12/100. Identify the plateau, describe the change occurring there, and explain what the continued energy supply is doing. **Expected answer:** The plateau is 8–12 minutes at 100°C; liquid changes to gas; energy enables particles to separate rather than raising temperature during the change. **Scoring:** 1 for interval, 1 for temperature, 1 for state change, 1 for explanation. **Feedback:** A flat section does not mean the heat source stopped. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

### B3. Compare before and after — 4 marks
Outcome 1.2(c). **Task:** Record P: ice melts and can be refrozen. Record Q: iron develops rust with properties different from the starting iron. Classify P and Q and give one evidence-based reason for each. **Expected answer:** P is physical: the substance remains water; Q is chemical: a new substance forms. **Scoring:** 1 for each classification and 1 for each supporting reason. Reversibility alone is insufficient evidence for every possible change. **Feedback:** Ask whether the substance’s identity changed; a change being difficult to reverse does not by itself prove it is chemical. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

## Instrument C — Applications and decisions, 15 minutes, 10 marks

### C1. Physical or chemical? — 4 marks
Outcome 1.2(c). **Task:** Classify melting candle wax and burning candle wax. Explain one difference between the changes. **Expected answer:** Melting is physical; burning is chemical. Melted wax remains wax, whereas burning produces different substances. **Scoring:** 1 per classification and 2 for the comparison using substance identity. Do not require learners to name all combustion products. **Feedback:** One material can undergo different types of change. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

### C2. Match a change to its use — 4 marks
Outcome 1.2(d). **Task:** For each situation, name the state change and explain its use: wet clothes drying; liquid water frozen into ice for cooling food. **Expected answer:** Evaporation changes liquid water to gas and dries clothing; freezing changes water to solid ice, which can absorb energy as it later melts and cools nearby food. **Scoring:** 1 for each named change and 1 for each useful explanation. Accept simple age-appropriate cooling language. **Feedback:** Name both the change and the purpose it serves. Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

### C3. Make a useful choice — 2 marks
Outcome 1.2(e). **Task:** A household wants to dry washed grain. Propose one way to encourage water to evaporate and explain why the choice is useful. **Expected evidence:** Spreading grain in a thin layer in a clean, ventilated place, with a reason such as exposing more water to moving air and helping drying. **Scoring:** 1 for a feasible method and 1 for its state-change explanation. Accept another sound method without unsafe heating. **Feedback:** Link the proposed action to evaporation rather than saying only “it dries faster.” Source: KICD table 1.2, printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 15–16.

## Coverage and marking record

1.2(a): A1, A2, B2. 1.2(b): B1. 1.2(c): B3, C1. 1.2(d): C2. 1.2(e): C3. Instrument totals: A = 8, B = 12, C = 10; pack total = 30. Treat C3 as evidence of a reasoned application; it does not establish the learner’s internal attitude.
