# The Cell — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Sub-strand 2.1 · Suggested teaching allocation: 20 lessons. Total: 32 marks. Use the structure check, microscope observation, and application check at separate points during teaching. Learners may use a microscope, teacher-provided micrographs, enlarged drawings, or tactile diagrams; record the mode used so observation skill and interpretation are distinguished.

Materials: light microscope, teacher-prepared plant slide, permanent animal-cell slide or clear micrograph, clean lens tissue, and pencils. The teacher handles glass preparation and stains. Carry microscopes with two hands, begin with low power, and keep liquids away from electrical parts. Use prepared images if equipment is unavailable. Do not collect cells from learners. Outcome references join the sub-strand number to the printed outcome letter.

## Instrument A — Structure and function, 15 minutes, 14 marks

### A1. Identify the cells — 4 marks
Outcomes 2.1(a), 2.1(c). **Task:** Drawing P has a rigid outer boundary, a large central vacuole, cytoplasm, and a nucleus. Drawing Q has a cell membrane, cytoplasm, and a nucleus, with no cell wall shown. Identify each as plant or animal and give one supporting feature. **Expected answer:** P is plant, supported by its wall or large central vacuole; Q is animal, supported by the absence of a cell wall in the supplied drawing. **Scoring:** 1 per identification and 1 per relevant evidence. **Feedback:** Use observed structures rather than cell shape alone. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

### A2. Link a component to its role — 6 marks
Outcome 2.1(b). **Task:** Give one function each for nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, cell wall, chloroplast, and large plant vacuole. **Expected answer:** Control of cell activities; site of many chemical reactions; control of movement into/out of cell; support; photosynthesis/light capture; storage of cell sap and support through turgor. **Scoring:** 1 per scientifically sound function. Accept age-appropriate equivalents; do not require all details listed. **Feedback:** Match a function to the named structure; the wall and membrane perform different roles. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

### A3. Compare, rather than list — 4 marks
Outcome 2.1(c). **Task:** State two structures shared by typical plant and animal cells and two structural differences. **Expected answer:** Shared examples: membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus. Differences: plant cells have a cell wall and usually a large permanent vacuole; many photosynthetic plant cells have chloroplasts, which animal cells lack. **Scoring:** 1 per shared structure and 1 per correct paired difference. Do not accept “all plant cells have chloroplasts.” **Feedback:** A comparison identifies what both have and what differs. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

## Instrument B — Microscope observation, 20 minutes, 13 marks

### B1. Work safely and systematically — 4 marks
Outcome 2.1(a). **Task:** Demonstrate or describe how to carry the microscope, place the prepared slide, begin observation, and bring the image into focus. **Expected evidence:** Two-hand carrying; slide secure on stage; low-power objective first; careful focusing while avoiding contact between objective and slide. **Scoring:** 1 per demonstrated/described step. The teacher intervenes immediately if glass or equipment is at risk. For an image-only alternative, record that microscope operation was described rather than observed. **Feedback:** Begin with a wide field of view and protect the slide. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

### B2. Make an observation drawing — 5 marks
Outcome 2.1(a). **Task:** Observe the supplied plant slide or micrograph. Draw two adjacent cells, label structures you can actually distinguish, and state one feature you cannot confidently see. **Expected evidence:** Clear adjacent outlines, proportions reflecting the view, labels attached to visible features, and an honest limit such as a nucleus not visible in that preparation. **Scoring:** adjacent cells 1; faithful outlines/proportions 1; two correct visible labels 2; valid observation limit 1. Accept an oral/tactile description with the same evidence. **Feedback:** Draw observations rather than adding every structure from memory. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

### B3. Calculate magnification — 4 marks
Outcome 2.1(d). **Task:** A microscope has a ×10 eyepiece. Find total magnification with (i) a ×4 objective and (ii) a ×40 objective. Show the rule used. **Expected answer:** Total magnification = eyepiece × objective; ×40 and ×400. **Scoring:** 1 per multiplication setup and 1 per answer. Magnification is a factor, so no millimetre unit is required. **Feedback:** Add neither the lens values nor a length unit. Source: KICD table 2.1 and Mathematics link, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

## Instrument C — Explain the importance, 10 minutes, 5 marks

### C1. Choose the first view — 2 marks
Outcomes 2.1(a), 2.1(d). **Task:** Explain why a learner begins with the low-power objective before changing to high power. **Expected answer:** Low power gives a wider field, making the specimen easier to locate and centre; high power then shows more detail of the selected region. **Scoring:** 1 for locating/centring with a wider view, 1 for the later detail purpose. **Feedback:** More magnification is useful only when the specimen is in view. Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

### C2. Cells and a living organism — 3 marks
Outcome 2.1(e). **Task:** A damaged leaf develops new tissue as it grows. Explain why studying cells helps someone understand this change. **Expected evidence:** Cells are basic living units; growth and repair involve cells; knowing structures and functions helps explain how tissue carries out its work. **Scoring:** 1 per clear idea, up to 3. Accept a relevant animal growth/repair example with the same reasoning. **Feedback:** Explain a connection between cells and an organism’s activity instead of saying only “cells are important.” Source: KICD table 2.1, printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 21–22.

## Coverage and marking record

2.1(a): A1, B1, B2, C1. 2.1(b): A2. 2.1(c): A1, A3. 2.1(d): B3, C1. 2.1(e): C2. Instrument totals: A = 14, B = 13, C = 5; pack total = 32. Record whether B1 was performed or described and whether B2 used a live microscope view or a provided image.
