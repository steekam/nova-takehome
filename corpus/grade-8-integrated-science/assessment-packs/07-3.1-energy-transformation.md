# Transformation of Energy — Assessment Pack

Grade 8 Integrated Science · Strand 3.0: Force and Energy · Sub-strand 3.1. Curriculum reference: KICD, *Integrated Science Grade 8 - July 2024*, table 3.1, printed pages 16–18 / PDF pages 28–30. Outcome references such as 3.1(a) combine the sub-strand number and printed letter and link to catalogue targets.

Use a 20-minute forms-and-sources check (12 marks), a supervised low-voltage investigation (14 marks), and a 20-minute application check (14 marks). Total: 40 marks. Materials: picture cards, battery, switch, teacher-inspected wires, small bulb or LED module, buzzer or small motor, toy car or ball, and a low stable ramp. Keep all circuits battery-powered; use no mains electricity, heating coils, flames or direct viewing of the Sun/bright lamps. Keep sounds low and ramps clear. Provide circuit photographs and teacher demonstration for learners who cannot manipulate equipment; accept spoken or diagram responses while recording hands-on evidence separately.

## Instrument A — Forms and sources

### A1. Identify energy forms — 6 marks
Outcome: 3.1(a); locator: table 3.1(a), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Name one relevant energy form for each: a moving ball; a raised book; a dry cell; a ringing bell; a lit torch; a cup of warm water. **Expected:** Kinetic; gravitational potential; chemical; sound; light; thermal/heat. Accept an additional form only if the learner explains its relevance to the example. **Scoring:** 1 per correct match, maximum 6. Do not credit “electricity” for the dry cell's stored energy without identifying chemical storage. **Feedback:** Ask whether the example stores energy, transfers energy, or shows an observable output.

### A2. Sort sources — 6 marks
Outcome: 3.1(b); locator: table 3.1(b), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Sort sunlight, wind, flowing water, coal, petroleum and natural gas into renewable and non-renewable sources. Explain the distinction and give one practical limit to a renewable supply at a particular place or time. **Expected:** Sunlight, wind and flowing water are renewable; coal, petroleum and natural gas are non-renewable. Renewable sources are replenished naturally on a useful human timescale; fossil fuels take very long periods to form. Supply may vary with daylight, weather or river flow. **Scoring:** 0.5 per correct source (3), 2 for the distinction, 1 for a relevant supply limit. **Feedback:** Ask how quickly nature replaces the source compared with how quickly people use it.

## Instrument B — Demonstrate a transformation

### B1. A battery-powered device — 6 marks
Outcomes: 3.1(c), 3.1(d); locator: table 3.1(c–d), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** With a teacher-inspected low-voltage circuit, operate a bulb, buzzer or motor. Draw the energy chain from the dry cell to the observed output, then demonstrate two safety steps. **Expected evidence:** Chemical energy in the cell transfers electrically to light, sound or movement, with some thermal energy also produced. The learner follows the diagram, avoids short circuits, keeps hands dry, disconnects after use, and reports hot/damaged parts. **Scoring:** 2 for correct chain, 2 for observable operation/record, 2 for distinct appropriate safety actions. An explained teacher-operated demonstration can earn explanation marks; record practical operation separately. **Feedback:** Ask where the energy begins and which outputs the learner actually observed.

### B2. A ball on a ramp — 4 marks
Outcome: 3.1(c); locator: table 3.1(c), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Release a ball from rest on a low stable ramp, without pushing. Describe the change in energy as it moves down and one observation showing the change. **Expected:** Gravitational potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases; the ball begins moving or gains speed. Some energy transfers to the surroundings as sound and thermal energy. **Scoring:** 2 for the main transformation, 1 for an observation, 1 for recognising a transfer to surroundings. Do not require a calculation. **Feedback:** Ask what differs between the ball at rest at the top and the same ball lower down.

### B3. Compare two releases — 4 marks
Outcomes: 3.1(c), 3.1(d); locator: table 3.1(c–d), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Plan a fair comparison of the same ball released from two safe heights on the same ramp. State what to change, what to keep alike, what to observe, and one safety measure. **Expected:** Change release height; keep ball/ramp/release method alike; observe a relevant movement measure such as travel distance on the same level surface; keep people and fragile items out of its path. **Scoring:** 1 for each requested element. Credit another workable observation and safe procedure. **Feedback:** Ask how a push or a different ball would make the comparison harder to interpret.

## Instrument C — Apply and evaluate

### C1. Repair the safety plan — 6 marks
Outcome: 3.1(d); locator: table 3.1(d), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Correct three plans: looking directly at the Sun to study light; testing a loudspeaker at maximum volume near a learner's ear; connecting a classroom circuit to a wall socket. For each, give the hazard and a safer method. **Expected:** Eye damage—use a safe indirect representation; hearing damage—low volume and distance; electric shock/fire—teacher-inspected low-voltage battery circuit. **Scoring:** 1 for each hazard and 1 for its suitable correction. **Feedback:** Ask whether the safer method still supplies the observation needed for learning.

### C2. Explain an everyday device — 4 marks
Outcomes: 3.1(c), 3.1(e); locator: table 3.1(c), 3.1(e), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** Choose a solar-powered lamp or a hand-powered dynamo lamp. Explain the main energy changes and one benefit of the device in daily life. **Expected:** Solar lamp: light to electrical energy, often stored chemically in a rechargeable battery, then electrical to light. Dynamo lamp: movement/mechanical energy to electrical energy, then light. A relevant benefit is lighting where mains power is unavailable. **Scoring:** 3 for a coherent chain and 1 for a linked benefit. Do not require battery storage where the selected device has none. **Feedback:** Ask the learner to explain every arrow in the energy chain.

### C3. Choose a source for a school task — 4 marks
Outcomes: 3.1(b), 3.1(e); locator: table 3.1(b), 3.1(e), printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30. **Task:** A school needs lighting for an evening study space. Compare solar with battery storage and a fuel generator using two practical criteria, then justify a choice. **Expected evidence:** A reasoned comparison may consider sunlight/site, storage, running cost, fuel supply, noise, fumes, maintenance or reliability. Both choices need safe installation and use; no source is best in every context. **Scoring:** 2 for two relevant comparisons, 1 for a choice supported by them, 1 for a limitation or safety condition. **Feedback:** Ask which local observation or cost information would strengthen the recommendation.

## Coverage and recording

3.1(a): A1. 3.1(b): A2, C3. 3.1(c): B1, B2, B3, C2. 3.1(d): B1, B3, C1. 3.1(e): C2, C3. Instrument totals: A 12 + B 14 + C 14 = 40 marks. Record observed performance and the explanation separately; use the application answers as evidence of informed choices rather than a claim about a learner's private attitude.
