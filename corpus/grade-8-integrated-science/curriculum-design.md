# Grade 8 Integrated Science — Curriculum Design Map

This file organizes the parts of the KICD design used by the linked Grade 8 schemes and assessment packs. It is a concise paraphrase, not a transcript. The KICD PDF remains the authority for the full outcome wording. Outcome references below combine the printed sub-strand number and outcome letter; they are source locators, not standalone outcome records.

For Arabic printed pages in the PDF, add 12 to get the PDF page number. For example, printed pages 1–2 appear on PDF pages 13–14.

## Delivery guidance

- The design provides five 40-minute Integrated Science lessons per week (printed page ix / PDF page 9).
- It recommends an inquiry-based 5E learning cycle (printed page xi / PDF page 11).
- Suggested lesson allocations are adaptable to context. KICD does not prescribe a term-by-term split (printed page xii / PDF page 12).
- The local schemes use three 10-week terms with five lessons per week. That 50-lessons-per-term pacing is a planning assumption, not a KICD allocation.

## Outcome scope map

### 1.1 Elements and Compounds — 18 lessons

Table 1.1, printed pages 1–2 / PDF pages 13–14.

- `1.1(a)` — Relate atoms, elements, molecules and compounds.
- `1.1(b)` — Use symbols for selected elements.
- `1.1(c)` — Represent selected reactions that form compounds with word equations.
- `1.1(d)` — Describe common uses of elements in society.
- `1.1(e)` — Appreciate information on labels of commonly used products.

### 1.2 Physical and chemical changes — 22 lessons

Table 1.2, printed pages 3–4 / PDF pages 15–16.

- `1.2(a)` — Explain particle arrangement and movement in solids, liquids and gases.
- `1.2(b)` — Compare how impurities affect melting and boiling behaviour.
- `1.2(c)` — Distinguish physical changes from chemical changes using evidence.
- `1.2(d–e)` — Apply state-change ideas in everyday contexts; use the cited outcome locators for the full wording of each letter.

### 1.3 Classes of fire — 20 lessons

Table 1.3, printed pages 5–6 / PDF pages 17–18.

- `1.3(a)` — Identify natural causes of fire.
- `1.3(b)` — Explain how the fire triangle relates to fire spread.
- `1.3(c)` — Select suitable control principles for different classes of fire.
- `1.3(d)` — Describe dangers that fire can pose to nature.

### 2.1 The Cell — 20 lessons

Table 2.1, printed pages 9–10 / PDF pages 21–22.

- `2.1(a)` — Observe and identify plant and animal cell structures with light microscopy.
- `2.1(b)` — Relate cell components to their functions.
- `2.1(c)` — Compare plant and animal cells.
- `2.1(d)` — Determine total microscope magnification.
- `2.1(e)` — Explain the role of cells in living things.

### 2.2 Movement of Materials in and out of the cell — 16 lessons

Table 2.2, printed pages 11–12 / PDF pages 23–24.

- `2.2(a)` — Explain diffusion and osmosis.
- `2.2(b)` — Demonstrate these movements using living material or a suitable model.
- `2.2(c)` — Describe their roles in living things, including water uptake, absorption and gas exchange.
- `2.2(d)` — Explain why these movements matter to living things.

### 2.3 Reproduction in human beings — 18 lessons

Table 2.3, printed pages 13–14 / PDF pages 25–26.

- `2.3(a)` — Describe the menstrual cycle and related challenges.
- `2.3(b)` — Develop practical plans for managing menstrual challenges.
- `2.3(c)` — Explain fertilisation and implantation.
- `2.3(d)` — Recognize possible symptoms associated with the named sexually transmitted infections.
- `2.3(e)` — Describe ways to prevent sexually transmitted infections.
- `2.3(f)` — Recognize the value of reproductive health.

### 3.1 Transformation of Energy — 20 lessons

Table 3.1, printed pages 16–18 / PDF pages 28–30.

- `3.1(a)` — Identify forms of energy in nature and familiar contexts.
- `3.1(b)` — Classify energy sources as renewable or non-renewable.
- `3.1(c)` — Demonstrate simple energy transformations.
- `3.1(d)` — Apply safety measures when using or observing energy transformations.
- `3.1(e)` — Explain useful energy transformations in daily life.

### 3.2 Pressure — 16 lessons

Table 3.2, printed pages 19–20 / PDF pages 31–32.

- `3.2(a)` — Demonstrate pressure effects in solids and liquids.
- `3.2(b)` — Identify pressure applications in everyday settings.
- `3.2(c)` — Appreciate useful pressure applications in everyday life.

## Scope and safe-delivery notes

- `1.2`: Heating and boiling comparisons require burn prevention and teacher control of heat sources.
- `1.3`: Fire causes, classes and controls can be explored with scenarios and diagrams; the linked scheme avoids live-fire practice.
- `2.1`: Use prepared slides, plant material or images; the linked scheme does not use learner cheek-cell samples.
- `2.3`: Omit hormonal-control detail and detailed blastocyst formation. Cover HIV/AIDS, gonorrhoea, syphilis and herpes symptoms and prevention without explaining causative agents. Use fictional cases and do not solicit private health disclosures.
- `3.1`: Address road, fire, electrical, bright-light and loud-sound hazards when exploring transformations.
- `3.2`: Keep liquid-pressure work qualitative; no quantitative pressure formula is required by this scope.
