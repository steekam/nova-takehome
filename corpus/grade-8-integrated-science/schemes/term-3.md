# Grade 8 Integrated Science — Term 3 Scheme of Work

Kenya CBC · Junior School · 10 teaching weeks · 50 lessons · five 40-minute lessons per week.

Reference design: KICD, [Integrated Science Grade 8 — July 2024](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RpqZRmeMywbc1Tya0_x-tNJBs75Gy0-v/preview), linked from the [Grade Eight Designs page](https://kicd.ac.ke/cbc-materials/curriculum-designs/grade-eight-designs/). KICD gives suggested lessons by sub-strand; this three-term placement is a school planning assumption. The term completes 2.3, then covers 3.1 and 3.2.

Outcome references join the sub-strand number to its printed outcome letter and link to existing catalogue records. Source locators: table 2.3, printed 13–14 / PDF 25–26; table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30; table 3.2, printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32. Weekly lesson numbers are within that week; term periods run from 1 to 50. Reproductive-health discussion uses fictional scenarios and includes no personal disclosures, hormonal-control detail or detailed blastocyst formation. Liquid pressure remains qualitative.

### Week 1 · Term periods 1–5 · 2.3 Reproduction in human beings
L1: Retrieve the menstrual-cycle sequence from Term 2, 2.3(a). L2: Interpret fictional examples of pain, irregular periods or irregular bleeding, 2.3(a). L3: Identify helpful hygiene, supplies, rest, support and health-service actions, 2.3(b). L4: Build a practical management plan for a fictional learner, 2.3(b). L5: Review plans for feasibility, privacy and respectful language, 2.3(b,f). Teacher uses vetted health information, makes clear that challenges deserve support, and gives a private route for questions. Resources: cycle and scenario cards; offer oral or written planning. Evidence: a workable plan with appropriate support-seeking. Source: table 2.3, printed 13–14 / PDF 25–26.

### Week 2 · Term periods 6–10 · 2.3 Reproduction in human beings
L1: Locate relevant reproductive structures on a labelled chart, 2.3(c). L2: Sequence sperm–egg fusion and later implantation, 2.3(c). L3: Explain the sequence using a simple diagram, 2.3(c). L4: Read age-appropriate information cards on symptoms associated with the named STIs, 2.3(d). L5: Explain why symptoms alone cannot establish a diagnosis and why care should be sought, 2.3(d,f). Teacher omits causative-agent detail and notes that an infection may have no obvious symptoms. Resources: charts and vetted information cards; use described diagrams. Evidence: correct sequence and a safe health-information response. Source: table 2.3, printed 13–14 / PDF 25–26.

### Week 3 · Term periods 11–15 · 2.3 → 3.1
L1: Match prevention actions to STI-risk scenarios, 2.3(e). L2: Correct misinformation using the supplied health sources, 2.3(d,e). L3: Create a respectful reproductive-health information message, 2.3(f). L4: Complete the sub-strand assessment and improve the management plan, 2.3(a–f). L5: Identify forms of energy in familiar objects and events, 3.1(a). Teacher addresses misinformation without stigma and avoids asking about learners' experiences. Resources: health cards, a simple energy picture set and response sheets; accept spoken responses. Evidence: justified prevention choices, supported health message and energy labels. Allocation: four lessons to 2.3 and one to 3.1. Sources: table 2.3, printed 13–14 / PDF 25–26; table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30.

### Week 4 · Term periods 16–20 · 3.1 Transformation of Energy
L1: Distinguish energy forms from sources using picture cards, 3.1(a,b). L2: Identify potential (including gravitational potential), kinetic, chemical, electrical, light, heat, sound and nuclear/atomic examples, 3.1(a). L3: Classify solar, wind, flowing water and biomass sources as renewable, and coal, petroleum and natural gas as non-renewable, 3.1(b). L4: Research one local energy-use context using vetted material, 3.1(b,e). L5: Explain and revise classification decisions, 3.1(b). Teacher probes replenishment timescales and checks the distinction between a source and a form. Resources: picture cards and source extracts, with accessible text equivalents. Evidence: justified classifications and correctly named forms. Source: table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30.

### Week 5 · Term periods 21–25 · 3.1 Transformation of Energy
L1: Trace chemical-to-electrical energy in a battery circuit, 3.1(c). L2: Build or observe electrical-to-light change in a low-voltage lamp, 3.1(c). L3: Compare electrical-to-sound in a buzzer and electrical-to-kinetic in a motor, 3.1(c). L4: Observe mechanical-to-electrical change in a hand dynamo and use a supplied heater record for electrical-to-heat change, 3.1(c). L5: Draw and explain the energy chains, 3.1(c). Teacher checks battery circuits and keeps mains electricity inaccessible. Resources: low-voltage kit, dynamo or images/data; offer switch-operated or observation roles. Evidence: chains with correct inputs, outputs and observed evidence. Source: table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30.

### Week 6 · Term periods 26–30 · 3.1 Transformation of Energy
L1: Model gravitational-potential-to-kinetic change using a ball on a low ramp, 3.1(c). L2: Rub hands gently to observe mechanical/kinetic-to-heat change, then compare it with a device energy chain, 3.1(c,e). L3: Identify electrical, fire, bright-light, loud-sound and road hazards in scenarios, 3.1(d). L4: Revise a demonstration plan to control hazards, 3.1(c,d). L5: Explain a useful transformation and safe use, 3.1(d,e). Teacher uses low heights, clear spaces and volume limits; learners can observe instead of rubbing hands. Resources: ramp, ball, device cards and safety scenarios, or recorded observations. Evidence: accurate chains and specific controls. Source: table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30.

### Week 7 · Term periods 31–35 · 3.1 → 3.2
L1: Compare two energy-use choices for a defined need, 3.1(b,e). L2: Prepare a safe-use advice card for a selected device, 3.1(d,e). L3: Complete the energy assessment, 3.1(a–e). L4: Correct explanations using feedback and evidence, 3.1(c–e). L5: Predict how the same load acts on different contact areas, 3.2(a). Teacher asks for supported choices rather than a single universally best energy source. Resources: scenario cards, assessment sheets, blocks and clay/sand in a tray. Evidence: justified choice, safe-use advice and pressure prediction. Allocation: four lessons to 3.1 and one to 3.2. Sources: table 3.1, printed 16–18 / PDF 28–30; table 3.2, printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32.

### Week 8 · Term periods 36–40 · 3.2 Pressure
L1: Compare marks made by a block resting on different faces, 3.2(a). L2: Change load while keeping contact area constant, 3.2(a). L3: Explain applications in footwear, broad straps and cutting tools, 3.2(b). L4: Predict liquid pressure at different depths using a teacher-prepared bottle demonstration, 3.2(a). L5: Compare observations with predictions qualitatively, 3.2(a). Teacher prepares holes and contains spills; no learner handles blades or sharp tools. Resources: blocks, clay, trays and water bottle, or an observation record. Evidence: fair comparisons and a depth-based liquid-pressure explanation; accept tactile or spoken records. Source: table 3.2, printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32.

### Week 9 · Term periods 41–45 · 3.2 Pressure
L1: Compare liquid demonstrations with the same liquid at different heights, 3.2(a). L2: Explain a needle-free linked-syringe model, 3.2(a,b). L3: Match everyday applications to pressure ideas, 3.2(b). L4: Trace the use of pressure in a supplied car-brake or pump diagram, 3.2(b). L5: Explain how an application helps people, 3.2(c). Teacher keeps pressure low, uses water only, and checks tubing connections. Resources: needle-free syringes and tubing or diagrams; no blowing through shared equipment. Evidence: an application explanation linked to contact area, load or liquid behaviour. Source: table 3.2, printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32.

### Week 10 · Term periods 46–50 · 3.2 Pressure
L1: Design a solution for spreading a load over soft ground, 3.2(b,c). L2: Test a model and record evidence, 3.2(a,b). L3: Improve the design and explain the change, 3.2(b,c). L4: Complete the pressure assessment, 3.2(a–c). L5: Review the year's evidence and correct one pressure misconception, 3.2(a–c). Teacher provides equal loads and a simple comparison method; drawings or supplied data can replace physical construction. Resources: card, blocks, trays and assessment pack. Evidence: a tested design, a supported improvement and an accurate pressure explanation. Source: table 3.2, printed 19–20 / PDF 31–32.
