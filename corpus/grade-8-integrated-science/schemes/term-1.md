# Grade 8 Integrated Science — Term 1 Scheme of Work

Kenya CBC · Junior School · 10 teaching weeks · 50 lessons · five 40-minute lessons per week.

Reference design: KICD, [Integrated Science Grade 8 — July 2024](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1RpqZRmeMywbc1Tya0_x-tNJBs75Gy0-v/preview), available through the [Grade Eight Designs page](https://kicd.ac.ke/cbc-materials/curriculum-designs/grade-eight-designs/). KICD gives suggested lessons by sub-strand. The three 10-week terms and the placement of topics below are school planning assumptions. Lessons include formative assessment; no additional examination periods are assumed.

Outcome references such as 1.1(a) join the sub-strand number to the source's printed outcome letter. They link to existing catalogue outcomes. Source locators: 1.1, table on printed pages 1–2 / PDF pages 13–14; 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16; 1.3, printed 5–6 / PDF 17–18. Weekly lesson numbers below are within that week; term periods run from 1 to 50.

### Week 1 · Term periods 1–5 · 1.1 Elements and Compounds
L1: Sort familiar materials and explain the evidence used, 1.1(a). L2: Build atom and element models using counters, 1.1(a). L3: Compare a molecule of an element with a compound model, 1.1(a). L4: Draw and classify supplied particle diagrams, 1.1(a). L5: Explain a new diagram to a partner and revise errors, 1.1(a). Teacher checks that learners separate particles from bulk materials. Resources: counters, bottle tops and diagram cards; offer tactile models and spoken descriptions. Evidence: a correctly labelled model and a reason for each classification. Source: table 1.1, printed 1–2 / PDF 13–14.

### Week 2 · Term periods 6–10 · 1.1 Elements and Compounds
L1: Match common element names to uses, 1.1(d). L2: Learn symbols for oxygen, carbon, hydrogen and nitrogen, 1.1(b). L3: Extend to selected metals and other listed elements, 1.1(b). L4: Correct symbol cards, paying attention to capital and lower-case letters, 1.1(b). L5: Use a supplied element list to identify elements named on clean product labels, 1.1(b,e). Teacher models one example then checks independent work. Resources: element chart and empty labelled packaging; provide enlarged copies. Evidence: symbol matching and one explanation of why a symbol is useful. Source: table 1.1, printed 1–2 / PDF 13–14.

### Week 3 · Term periods 11–15 · 1.1 Elements and Compounds
L1: Identify the elements in named two-element compounds, 1.1(a,c). L2: Arrange word cards for the formation of sodium chloride, water and carbon dioxide, 1.1(c). L3: Write word equations for copper oxide and aluminium oxide, then explain reactants and products across the five examples, 1.1(c). L4: Research uses of iron, copper, aluminium and other common elements, 1.1(d). L5: Present a use–property connection and complete a short equation check, 1.1(c,d). Teacher supplies vetted reaction descriptions; learners do not burn or react elements. Resources: word cards, labelled objects and print extracts or offline images. Evidence: correct reactant/product names and supported uses. Source: table 1.1, printed 1–2 / PDF 13–14.

### Week 4 · Term periods 16–20 · 1.1 → 1.2
L1: Read ingredients and safety information on clean packaging, 1.1(e). L2: Compare two labels and explain a useful consumer decision, 1.1(d,e). L3: Complete the elements-and-compounds checkpoint and correct errors, 1.1(a–e). L4: Observe solids, liquids and gases through safe objects or images, 1.2(a). L5: Build particle arrangements for each state, 1.2(a). Teacher draws attention to spacing, arrangement and movement; particle size stays consistent in the model. Resources: packaging, counters, state cards; give text descriptions. Evidence: label interpretation plus an explained particle model. Allocation: three lessons to 1.1 and two to 1.2. Sources: table 1.1, printed 1–2 / PDF 13–14; table 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16.

### Week 5 · Term periods 21–25 · 1.2 Physical and chemical changes
L1: Describe motion and spacing in the three particle models, 1.2(a). L2: Predict what happens when ink is placed in still water, 1.2(a). L3: Observe diffusion, record changes and compare prediction with evidence, 1.2(a). L4: Interpret a supplied heating curve and locate changes of state, 1.2(a). L5: Explain heating and cooling using particle movement, 1.2(a,d). Teacher asks learners to distinguish observation from explanation. Resources: water, food colouring or ink, transparent cups and printed curves; use recorded observations if colour access is limited. Evidence: annotated curve and explanation using particle movement. Source: table 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16.

### Week 6 · Term periods 26–30 · 1.2 Physical and chemical changes
L1: Plan a fair comparison of pure and impure substances, 1.2(b). L2: Read teacher-provided melting-point observations, 1.2(b). L3: Observe a supervised boiling comparison or use supplied data, 1.2(b). L4: Plot results and compare melting/boiling behaviour, 1.2(b). L5: Explain the impurity effect from the evidence, 1.2(b). Teacher handles heat sources, sets a safe viewing distance and supplies the data alternative before the lesson. Resources: thermometers and safe heating apparatus when available, or a printed results set. Evidence: labelled graph, controlled variables and a supported conclusion. Source: table 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16.

### Week 7 · Term periods 31–35 · 1.2 Physical and chemical changes
L1: Sort everyday change cards and justify initial decisions, 1.2(c). L2: Observe dissolving, melting or folding examples, 1.2(c). L3: Examine evidence from rusting, cooking or a teacher-supplied reaction record, 1.2(c). L4: Decide whether a new substance has formed and revise the card sort, 1.2(c). L5: Explain two contrasting changes in writing or orally, 1.2(c). Teacher challenges the rule that every irreversible change must be chemical. Resources: safe materials, images and evidence cards; no learner combustion activity. Evidence: classification supported by substance-change evidence rather than appearance alone. Source: table 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16.

### Week 8 · Term periods 36–40 · 1.2 Physical and chemical changes
L1: Trace evaporation and condensation in an everyday water-use example, 1.2(d). L2: Compare cooling, freezing and drying applications, 1.2(d,e). L3: Design a labelled explanation of a chosen application, 1.2(d,e). L4: Review peers' explanations for scientific accuracy and usefulness, 1.2(a,d,e). L5: Complete the sub-strand assessment and act on feedback, 1.2(a–e). Teacher supplies contexts such as food storage and drying without requiring home equipment. Resources: pictures, simple models and planning sheets; accept an oral model explanation. Evidence: a correct state-change sequence and a justified use. Source: table 1.2, printed 3–4 / PDF 15–16.

### Week 9 · Term periods 41–45 · 1.3 Classes of fire
L1: Identify natural and human-related causes in fire scenario cards, 1.3(a). L2: Build and explain a fire-triangle model, 1.3(b). L3: Predict how changing one triangle component affects burning, 1.3(b). L4: Trace how a small fire could spread through a pictured environment, 1.3(a,b,d). L5: Produce a danger map with evidence-based prevention messages, 1.3(d). Teacher uses images and paper models only; no flames or extinguishers are operated by learners. Resources: scenario cards, triangle pieces and maps; provide spoken descriptions. Evidence: cause–component–danger explanations. Source: table 1.3, printed 5–6 / PDF 17–18.

### Week 10 · Term periods 46–50 · 1.3 Classes of fire
L1: Group fire scenarios by burning material using the supplied fire-class guide, 1.3(c). L2: Link safe control principles to removal of heat, fuel or oxygen, 1.3(b,c). L3: Identify unsafe actions and explain the danger, 1.3(c,d). L4: Plan a fire-safety poster for a defined audience, 1.3(d). L5: Present the plan and record questions to revisit next term, 1.3(b–d). Teacher prioritises raising an alarm, moving to safety and adult/fire-service response; practice is through scenarios. Resources: class guide, pictures and poster materials. Evidence: correct control reasoning and a clear danger message. Source: table 1.3, printed 5–6 / PDF 17–18. Continue the remaining ten lessons in Term 2.

Term allocation check: 1.1 = 18 lessons; 1.2 = 22; 1.3 = 10. Total = 50 lessons = 2,000 minutes. Ten weeks each contain five lessons. Outcome-code coverage: 1.1(a–e), 1.2(a–e), 1.3(a–d), with 1.3 continued in Term 2. Formative checks are included in the allocated lessons.
